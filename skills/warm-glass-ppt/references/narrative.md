# Narrative modes

Choose by what the audience needs to do after the presentation. Use the same visual system across all four purposes, while changing the content structure. A roadmap or architecture page is a layout choice within a purpose, not a fifth narrative mode. The examples below are starting points, not fixed slide counts or mandatory outlines.

## Report · 汇报 · `report`

Help the audience assess progress or make a decision. Lead with the current conclusion and the decision required, then explain the supporting evidence. For a progress report, compare the agreed plan with actual results, explain material gaps and identify the next action, owner and date. For a proposal, show the problem, options, tradeoffs and recommendation.

Keep achievements, unresolved issues and planned work distinguishable. Quantified results need a period, baseline and definition. Put a material risk close to the conclusion it limits. End with a specific request for a decision or support when one is needed; an informational report can end with the next milestone. See `examples/report.json`.

## Talk · 演讲 · `talk`

Help an audience understand or reconsider one central idea. Start with a relevant question or concrete situation, establish why it matters, explain the idea through examples and evidence, and close with its implications. Connect technical detail to what changes for the audience. Choose depth and pacing from the audience and available time; the speaker's job title does not determine the structure.

A useful page script has: job, headline, supporting facts, visual relationship, spoken explanation and transition. Check the transition between neighboring pages before polishing wording. A short talk may need only a few pages; preserve any explicit page count. See `examples/talk.json`.

## Training · 培训 · `training`

Organize around what participants should understand, try and produce. Establish a concept before introducing a product. Connect trend pages to the learner's work; connect examples to the conditions that made the outcome possible. Pair opportunity with the relevant reliability or responsibility boundary.

An effective module moves from a concrete question through a visual model and a worked example to a small practice task. State the practice input, expected output and assessment criteria. Use a module divider when it helps the learning sequence, and make its learning goal clear. Reuse the visual language without copying the same layout for every concept. See `examples/training.json`.

## Product introduction · 产品介绍 · `product`

Help the intended user or buyer judge whether the product fits their work. Establish the target audience and their problem, explain the product's value, and show a representative workflow or scenario. Select features because they support that workflow. Add the relevant integration, reliability, security or adoption conditions, then offer an appropriate next step such as a trial, demonstration or requirements discussion.

Distinguish available capabilities, limited pilots and future plans. Source claims about performance, customers, differentiation and price; use labeled examples or placeholders when evidence is missing. Explain a comparison on a shared basis instead of inventing a competitor's shortcomings. See `examples/product.json`.

## Page ownership

Assign each important fact or metric a primary page. A case page explains the intervention and causal chain; a value page owns consolidated outcomes; the conclusion names the next decision. Related pages may reference the same topic without repeating identical copy and numbers. Speaker notes can carry supporting detail that would overload the projected slide.

Use current evidence for dates, regulations and product claims when research is part of the assignment. Attribute third-party claims in speaker notes. Keep measured outcomes, targets, assumptions and unverified figures visibly distinct in all four modes.
