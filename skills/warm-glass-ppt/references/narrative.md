# Narrative modes

## Executive talk

Start with the enterprise decision and the value at stake. Then explain the industry shift, the institution's constraints, the capabilities already built, the real use cases, the evidence and the next decisions. Engineering is one means of delivering enterprise value; show how it connects with business scenarios and changes in ownership or collaboration.

A 15–20 minute talk often works with roughly 12–18 pages, but preserve the user's explicit count. Give dense evidence more time than a transition. A useful page script has: job, headline, supporting facts, visual relationship, spoken explanation, and transition. Check the transition between every neighboring pair before polishing individual wording.

Example sequence, adapted to the supplied source rather than copied as a mandatory outline:

1. Enterprise objective and opening question.
2. Industry change and institution-specific constraints.
3. Capability, scenario and organizational implications.
4. Engineering practice and how it supports delivery.
5. Scenario discovery, use cases and measured value.
6. Next steps, responsibilities and closing judgment.

Use current evidence for dates, regulations and product claims when the assignment requires research. Attribute third-party claims in speaker notes. Keep measured outcomes, targets, assumptions and unverified figures visibly distinct.

## Training course

Organize around what participants should understand, try and produce. Establish a concept before introducing a product. Connect trend pages to the learner's work; connect examples to the conditions that made the outcome possible. Pair opportunity with the relevant reliability or responsibility boundary.

An effective module moves from a concrete question through a visual model and a worked example to a small practice task. State the practice input, expected output and assessment criteria. A training divider carries module number, question and learning goal. Reuse the visual language across modules without copying the same layout for every concept.

## Page ownership

Assign each important fact or metric a primary page. A case page explains the intervention and causal chain; a value page owns consolidated outcomes; the conclusion names the next decision. Related pages may reference the same topic without repeating identical copy and numbers. Speaker notes can carry supporting detail that would overload the projected slide.
